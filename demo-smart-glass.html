How it works

Three layers, one switch

ClearSwitch film uses PDLC technology, a layer of liquid crystals sandwiched between conductive layers, to control light instantly with a low-voltage current.

1

Applied to existing glass

A technician cleans the surface and applies the film directly to existing glass, or it's laminated between panes for new construction. No need to replace windows or partitions.

2

Wired to a low-voltage switch

Each panel connects to a small transformer and a wall switch, remote, or smart-home integration. Power draw is comparable to a small LED light.

3

Instant privacy on demand

Flip the switch and the crystals realign, turning the glass from frosted to clear (or back) in a fraction of a second, with no visible seams or moving parts.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How does switchable smart glass film actually work?

Smart film uses a layer of liquid crystals, called PDLC, sandwiched inside the film. With no power applied, the crystals scatter light in every direction, so the glass looks frosted and opaque. When a low-voltage current is applied, the crystals line up and let light pass straight through, so the glass turns clear almost instantly.

Can it be installed on existing glass, or does the glass need to be replaced?

In most cases the film can be applied directly to existing glass, so there is no need to replace windows or partitions. For new construction, the film can also be laminated between two panes of glass at the factory.

How much power does smart glass film use?

Smart film draws power only while it is switched to the clear state, comparable to a small LED light. A typical panel uses only a few watts per square meter, so running cost is minimal.
